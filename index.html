      How this site works

      Tap any symbol and it is copied to your clipboard; a bar at the bottom shows its official name, its Unicode number and its HTML code. Then go to your message, document or form and paste. On a phone, hold your finger in the text box and choose Paste; on a computer, press Ctrl + V, or Command + V on a Mac. The symbols you copied are remembered on this device only, so that they are at hand the next time you visit. Nothing is sent to us.

      Typing a symbol instead of copying it

      If you need a symbol every day, learn its shortcut. On Windows that is usually an Alt code or the symbol panel that opens with the Windows key and the full stop; see how to type symbols on Windows. A Mac has Option key shortcuts and a searchable viewer: how to type symbols on a Mac. Phone keyboards hide extra signs behind a long press on a key: symbols on Android and iPhone. People who write web pages will want the HTML entity codes.

      Questions

      Are these symbols free to use?

      Yes. They are characters of the Unicode standard, like letters and digits. Nobody owns a character. A few signs, such as ® and ©, have legal meaning, so use those correctly.

      Why does a symbol look different after I paste it?

      You copy the character, not its picture. The app where you paste draws it in its own font, and phones turn some symbols into coloured emoji. The reasons are explained in why symbols show as boxes or look different.

      Can I use them in a name on WhatsApp, Instagram or a game?

      Most apps accept them, but each has its own rules, and some strip unusual characters. Simple, old symbols such as ♥ ★ ✓ • → are the most widely accepted.

      Is there a rupee symbol on the keyboard?

      Many keyboards sold in India print ₹ on the 4 key. With the English (India) layout in Windows, Ctrl + Alt + 4 types it. The rupee sign page covers every device.
