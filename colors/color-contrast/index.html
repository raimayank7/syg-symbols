Colour contrast: making text easy to read

Pale grey text on a white page looks elegant and is hard to read. Yellow on white is worse. Whether words can be read depends less on the colours themselves than on the difference in brightness between the text and its background. That difference is called contrast, and it can be measured.

The contrast ratio

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), define a contrast ratio between two colours. It runs from 1 : 1, for two identical colours, to 21 : 1, for black and white. The higher the number, the easier the text is to read.

The numbers to aim for

Level AALevel AAA
Normal textat least 4.5 : 1at least 7 : 1
Large textat least 3 : 1at least 4.5 : 1
Icons, buttons and other controlsat least 3 : 1

Large text means at least 18 point, or at least 14 point if bold. Level AA is the level most rules and policies ask for. These figures are from WCAG 2.2 (success criteria 1.4.3, 1.4.6 and 1.4.11), in the W3C Recommendation dated 12 December 2024, read for this page on 12 November 2026. Logos and purely decorative text are exempt.

How the ratio is worked out

Each colour is first turned into a relative luminance between 0 (black) and 1 (white). The red, green and blue values are adjusted for the way screens work, and then added with the weights 0.2126 for red, 0.7152 for green and 0.0722 for blue. Green counts most because the eye is most sensitive to it. That is why pure yellow, which is red plus green, is very bright, and pure blue is very dark.

The ratio is then (L1 + 0.05) ÷ (L2 + 0.05), where L1 is the luminance of the lighter colour and L2 of the darker.

Practical rules

  • Dark text on a light background, or the reverse. Two middle colours never work, however different their hues.
  • Do not rely on colour alone. A red error message should also have an icon or the word "Error", because some people cannot tell red from green.
  • Be careful with text on photographs. Put a dark or light band behind the words.
  • Light grey text needs checking. On white, the lightest grey that reaches 4.5 : 1 is about #767676.
  • Bright does not mean readable. Pure yellow or lime on white fails badly. Pure blue on white passes easily.

Colour blindness

Many people, more men than women, see some colours differently, most often reds and greens. Good contrast in brightness helps them too, since brightness is seen even when hue is not. Charts and maps should add labels, patterns or shapes, and not use colour as the only sign.

Check your own colours

The contrast checker measures any two colours and shows which levels they pass. Every colour page on this site also says whether black or white text reads better on that colour.
