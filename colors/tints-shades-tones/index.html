Tints, shades and tones

From one colour you can make a whole family. Add white and it becomes lighter. Add black and it becomes darker. Add grey and it becomes softer. Artists have a name for each of these, and knowing them makes it much easier to build a palette that hangs together.

The three words

  • A tint is a colour mixed with white. Pink is a tint of red. Sky blue is a tint of blue.
  • A shade is a colour mixed with black. Maroon is a shade of red. Navy is a shade of blue.
  • A tone is a colour mixed with grey, that is, with both black and white. Tones are muted, dusty versions of a colour.

In everyday speech "shade" is used for all of these, as in "a nice shade of green". The strict meanings matter only when mixing.

The same idea in colour codes

In HSL codes these changes are easy to read. A tint raises the lightness above 50%. A shade lowers it below 50%. A tone lowers the saturation. The hue number does not change, which is why the family still looks like one colour.

Why they matter in design

A design that uses five different hues at full strength looks like a box of sweets. A design that uses one or two hues, each in several tints and shades, looks calm and planned. A common pattern for a website or a poster is:

  • a very light tint for the background,
  • the pure colour, or a slightly darker shade, for buttons and headings,
  • a very dark shade for text,
  • and one contrasting accent colour.

A scheme made this way from a single hue is called monochromatic. The palette maker builds one for any colour.

Mixing them in paint

  • Tints: start with white and add the colour a little at a time. Starting with the colour wastes a great deal of white.
  • Shades: add black in very small amounts. Black overpowers quickly. Many painters darken with a dark blue or brown, or with the opposite colour, because black can make a mixture look flat.
  • Tones: mix a grey first, then add it to the colour.
