Colour contrast checker

Type a text colour and a background colour. You get the contrast ratio, a sample of how it looks, and which accessibility levels it passes.

Reading the result

The ratio runs from 1 : 1 (no contrast at all) to 21 : 1 (black on white). The table under it compares your ratio with the figures in the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.2):

  • 4.5 : 1 for normal text and 3 : 1 for large text, at level AA, the level most often required.
  • 7 : 1 and 4.5 : 1 at the stricter level AAA.
  • 3 : 1 for icons, buttons, form borders and other things that must be seen to use the page.

Large text means at least 18 point, or 14 point bold. The figures were read from the W3C Recommendation of 12 December 2024 on 12 November 2026.

If your pair fails

  • Make the darker colour darker or the lighter colour lighter. Changing the hue alone rarely helps.
  • Open the text colour in the converter and try its darker shades.
  • Make the text larger or bolder, if 3 : 1 is within reach.
  • Put the words on a plain band if they sit on a photograph.

What the checker does not know

It compares two flat colours. It cannot judge very thin lettering, text over a picture or gradient, or a screen in bright sunlight, all of which need more contrast than the figures suggest. A pass here is a good sign, not a guarantee, and it is not a legal certificate of accessibility.
