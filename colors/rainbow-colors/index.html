The seven colours of the rainbow: VIBGYOR

A rainbow shows the colours of sunlight spread out in order. By tradition there are seven: violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange and red. The first letters spell VIBGYOR, the word Indian school children use to remember them. Read the other way, from the outside of the bow inwards, it is ROYGBIV.

How a rainbow forms

Sunlight looks white but is a mixture of all colours. When a ray of sunlight enters a raindrop, it bends. Each colour bends by a slightly different amount, violet the most and red the least, so the colours separate. The light then reflects off the back of the drop and bends again as it leaves. Millions of drops, each sending one colour to your eye, together make the bow.

That is why you see a rainbow only when the Sun is behind you and rain is falling in front of you, and why it is seen most often in the morning or the late afternoon, when the Sun is low.

The same thing with a prism

A triangular glass prism does what a raindrop does. Isaac Newton passed a beam of sunlight through a prism in the 1660s and saw it spread into a band of colours, which he called a spectrum. With a second prism he joined the colours back into white light, showing that the colours were in the sunlight all along and were not added by the glass.

Why seven?

The spectrum is a smooth band with no sharp lines between colours. You could name five colours in it or fifty. Newton chose seven, adding orange and indigo to the five he first listed. Many people cannot pick out indigo as a separate colour between blue and violet, and some modern books list only six.

Colours that are not in the rainbow

Pink, brown, magenta, grey, black and white are not in the rainbow. Each rainbow colour is a single wavelength of light. Magenta is what we see when red and blue light arrive together. Brown is a dark orange. White is all the colours at once, and black is no light at all.

Double rainbows

Sometimes a second, fainter bow appears outside the first. It is made by light that reflects twice inside each drop. Its colours are in the reverse order, with red on the inside.

Ways to remember the order

  • VIBGYOR from the inside of the bow outwards.
  • ROYGBIV, or the sentence "Richard Of York Gave Battle In Vain", from the outside inwards.
  • Make your own sentence with the seven first letters. A silly one is remembered best.

Make a rainbow at home

On a sunny morning, stand with your back to the Sun and spray a fine mist from a garden hose. A small rainbow appears in the mist. A glass of water on a sunny windowsill, or the edge of a CD, will also throw a band of colours on a wall.

See the colours one by one: violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange and red.
